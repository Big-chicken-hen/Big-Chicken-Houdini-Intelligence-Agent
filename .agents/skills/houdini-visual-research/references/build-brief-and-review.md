# Risk Routing and Professional Review

Use this contract to choose risk depth and route domain-specific professional evidence. Do not load the full workflow for one primitive, one known parameter or connection change, routine read-only inspection, or an ordinary API error. For every Full Goal, use the detailed user-visible blueprint and complete current-stage card in [full-goal-blueprint.md](full-goal-blueprint.md); when the resolved choice is project team, also use its five-Thread Panel project lifecycle. This reference does not replace that blueprint with a short Brief.

## Scale the workflow by risk

- **Direct:** execute a known deterministic edit immediately and perform one necessary targeted validation. Do not require local retrieval, a Full Blueprint, external research, capture, or artifact review.
- **Focused:** for a bounded multi-node edit or familiar technique, retrieve only when a node, parameter, version, project convention, or failure cause is uncertain. Use only the one or two relevant supports, such as narrow help, a compact semantic expectation, a scene diff, or one visual check.
- **Full:** use one shared batched local lookup when the task involves an uncertain or version-sensitive complex material/simulation/Solaris workflow, historical project convention, or uncertain failure cause. Add SideFX/original-source research only when an unresolved source, technique, licensing, renderer, or version question can materially change construction or acceptance; Full complexity alone does not trigger it. Publish the complete blueprint and stage cards, pass the `full-goal-blueprint.md` production information-depth and semantic checks, give Supervisor the complete artifact for initial authorization and material revisions, then acquire the applicable technical and visual professional evidence through bounded current-stage loops. A project-team outcome uses the five Panel project Threads; a single-AI outcome stays in the original Thread.

These are reasoning tiers, not a Gate, approval layer, state machine, or fixed tool sequence. Escalate or reduce the workflow when observed uncertainty, cost, or evidence changes. A direct color assignment remains Direct; material complexity or color-pipeline uncertainty, not the word “color” alone, raises the tier.

## Authorize the full blueprint before bounding the loop

For a project-team Full Goal, Planning keeps the authoritative target-specific
blueprint in its Panel project Thread and established Panel surface, and sends that
complete artifact to Supervisor for initial authorization and again after every
material revision. For an explicit single-AI Full Goal, keep and strictly review the
same artifact in the original Panel Thread. Even a sparse complete-asset request must
pass the Full reference's production floors of at least 10,000 task-specific
information units overall, 2,500 per complete stage card, and 350 per ordered step,
plus its task-anchor, required-structure, anti-repetition/filler, and semantic
completeness checks. This is a Houdini Full authorization contract, not an intermediate
representation, machine node list, general Planner, reusable Gate, or state machine.

Supervisor must receive the complete synthesized blueprint, all stage cards,
provenance, evidence plan, and production validation result for that authorization. A
pointer, short brief, or Planning assurance is insufficient. After authorization,
keep repeated execution and review turns bounded to the global hard-constraint
capsule, complete current stage card, and latest evidence delta. Reference long source
bodies and working transcripts rather than pasting them; do not confuse those raw
artifacts with the complete blueprint Supervisor is required to review. Internal
research returns may stay concise, but they must be synthesized into the detailed
authoritative blueprint rather than replacing it.

Do not remove or weaken a user requirement. Record explicit required and forbidden outcomes as User facts. A negative constraint is hard on the observed result and its semantic role, including an equivalent substitute produced by another node or method; only the user may relax it or authorize an approximation. If a Codex-originated recognition feature or completion promise proves infeasible, report it as incomplete or request user direction instead of rewriting acceptance to manufacture a pass.

## Turn knowledge into semantic expectations

Knowledge retrieval supplies implementation evidence; it does not prove the resulting network is correct. Before a Focused or Full write, distill only the retrieved rules that can change success into explicit semantic expectations: target path or scope, observed field/attribute/relationship, expected mapping or range, relevant frame or stage, tolerance when needed, and the evidence that would settle it.

Examples include source `flame` mapping to destination `flame`, required fields having a nonzero active range, and velocity remaining finite with magnitude plausible for the scene scale. After execution, ask the current `hia_validate` semantic-expectation capability to evaluate only these task-specific claims. Read its live tool contract at call time; do not restate MCP payload fields in the Skill or invent them from prose. An error-free cook does not satisfy a semantic expectation by itself.

If a semantic result is `unknown` or emits a `*_NOT_PROVEN` notice, expose that once as a real risk or unverified claim with the missing evidence. Do not repeat the same warning across every stage or convert absence of proof into success.

## Build in semantic stages

Advance in this order when applicable:

1. proportions and primary form;
2. structure, supports, contacts, and major negative spaces;
3. mid-level assemblies and functional components;
4. task-relevant detail;
5. materials and surface response;
6. lighting, animation, simulation, or FX that is actually in scope;
7. output and delivery validation.

Merge or skip inapplicable stages; this is not a fixed Gate or iteration count. At each visible milestone in a project-team Goal, compare technical and visual evidence with the complete current stage card, name the largest consequential deviation, let Supervisor approve the smallest coherent repair and only Execution apply it, then recheck the same unchanged claims through both parallel review Threads. Continue without a fixed iteration count until both applicable evidence sets are verified; stop earlier only for a user stop or genuine evidenced blocker, which remains incomplete rather than passing.

## Use the Panel project roles

For a project-team Full Goal, reuse exactly the five Threads defined in `full-goal-blueprint.md`: 监督（Supervisor） strictly authorizes the complete initial or materially revised blueprint and owns the evidence/rework loop, 方案（Planning） owns that full blueprint and stage cards, 执行（Execution） is the only live-HIP writer, and 视觉审查（Visual Review） plus 技术审查（Technical Review） independently review every stage in parallel. Any Panel-supported model may serve a role. When Supervisor, Planning, Visual Review, or Technical Review actually exposes native subagent tools, use them for genuinely parallel, non-overlapping read-only research or review; they never replace the five Threads or Execution boundary. Execution never proactively spawns or delegates to native subagents because they inherit its scene-write capability.

These are responsibilities within Codex collaboration, not persistent backend Agents, another model, a scheduler, or a local chat system. Project activation failure must remain visible rather than being mislabeled as a user-selected single-AI outcome. Missing either review Thread or any internal subagent must never collapse the asset into one all-system batch, lower the evidence bar, or shorten the blueprint.

Keep internal subtask returns compact: **Decision**, **Evidence**, **Risks**, **Recommended next action**, and **Unverified**. Synthesize them into the full blueprint or review ledger. Do not forward full working transcripts to Supervisor, but always forward the complete synthesized blueprint when initial or material-revision authorization is due.

## Route professional acceptance

Select only the domains needed by the current stage card:

| Domain | Judgment | Minimum useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modeling | Identity, proportions, hierarchy, construction, contacts, topology, and editability | Live paths and errors, geometry facts, representative view, and a relevant control/dependency check |
| Material | Material identity, bindings, primvars, texture/color handling, optical response, and displacement scale | Material summary or live paths plus a representative diagnostic or target view |
| Lighting and render | Camera intent, light direction, separation, exposure, shadows, renderer support, and delivery settings | Recorded frame/camera, light and render settings, and a representative capture or render |
| Animation | Timing, arcs, constraints, deformation, continuity, and target-frame readability | Relevant range, representative frames, live channel/constraint evidence, and observed pops or errors |
| Simulation | Scale, sourcing, collisions, stability, continuity, cache validity, and art direction | Solver/source/collision paths, reset and configured start evidence, a cook beginning at that start, representative phases or suspect interval, dependency evidence, errors, and cache/output coverage |
| Network organization | Semantic flow, names, outputs, crossings, dead experiments, and maintainable boundaries | Bounded read-only graph inspection of the affected network |
| Performance | Cook or frame cost, geometry and instance strategy, time dependence, cache behavior, and quality tradeoffs | Measured values or tool evidence from the relevant scope and an appropriate baseline; never invented estimates |

Do not turn review into a score or exhaustive checklist. Report the highest-impact issue first, its path or scope, evidence, severity, responsible role, minimum correction, and any missing proof. Read-only inspection does not trigger construction, a Full Blueprint, or a review-fix loop by itself.

For a simulation or cache completion claim, require evidence that the state was reset, cooking began from the configured start, the relevant range or representative phases were evaluated, the cache is current and covers the claimed output, and required upstream dependencies participated. Interpret the current `hia_validate` cook/cache evidence through its live runtime contract instead of freezing its output schema here. Any unproven item keeps the claim unverified; report the missing proof once rather than emitting a long repeated warning. Observed bounded recomputation does not by itself prove reset, range coverage, cache freshness, or dependency participation.

## Promote only a Verified Procedure

Call a workflow a **Verified Procedure** only when its relevant steps succeeded in real Houdini on a recorded build, the required technical or visual evidence supports the claim, and the user accepted the result. Only then may the Supervisor propose it for a formal knowledge entry or reusable workflow memory; promotion is still explicit, never automatic.

A failed or merely plausible attempt is not a Verified Procedure. Keep failures in the bounded diagnostic report or unverified experience notes, and do not automatically write them to project memory. If a later corrected workflow succeeds and is accepted, promote only the bounded successful procedure with its version, scope, evidence, and limitations.
